    Merged-blob ideas for 3D-PTV (brainstorm)¶

    Context: two_phase tracker is near-ceiling on reachable links (99 %). Ghosts cost 0.5–2 %. The real gap is merged blobs: 27 % of blob observations hold ≥2 particles (≈13 % on real wp2). The correspondence step assigns each blob to one 3D point, so a merged blob (a) shifts the kept particle, (b) drops the partner, and (c) the dropped partner then steals the merged blob as a "clean" observation and corrupts its partner. Membership inference is already ~98 % correct; the loss is from hidden partners not being recovered. Bringing them back also makes the already-tracked particles more accurate.

    Theme 1 — Representation: a blob is a container, not a point¶

    1. Blob-as-container state. Maintain per-blob a set/list of particle IDs it is believed to contain, updated by the tracking loop rather than thrown away after correspondence. The loop's unit of matching becomes blob↔blob (containers), with particles moving through containers; de-merging happens inside a container. This is the "wrapped in a loop that keeps track of what each blob contains" architecture.

    2. Multiplicity / super-particle points. A 3D point produced from a merged blob is not one particle but a cluster with multiplicity m. Store m (and an estimated per-constituent intensity) on the point. Downstream tracking treats it as a soft observation with inflated covariance instead of a clean point, preventing the "partner steals merged blob as clean" corruption.

    3. Intensity/area budget per particle. Track each particle's blob brightness (and area) as a conserved quantity. A merged blob's brightness ≈ sum of constituents. When a particle is occluded/merged, its brightness is absorbed into the host; recovery is triggered when a host's brightness exceeds its single-particle budget by a threshold.

    Theme 2 — De-merging at correspondence time¶

    1. Mixture-centroid solve. For a merged blob in camera c with measured centre μ_c and known/estimated constituent intensities {I_k}, and candidate particles with predicted positions, solve for updated positions such that the intensity-weighted projection of the constituents reproduces μ_c (plus other cameras' constraints). Small nonlinear least squares per merged blob; recovers both positions jointly and also improves the kept particle (matches "bringing them back makes existing particles more accurate").

    2. All-subsets triangulation. Allow the same 2D blob to participate in multiple candidate 3D points (with a penalty), instead of one-to-one assignment. Enumerate pairings of a merged blob with blobs in other cameras; generate candidate points for hidden partners. Let the tracker's consistency (trajectory continuity) select the subset. Cost: more candidates, but membership inference already filters.

    3. Residual peeling with known templates. After assigning the best point to a merged blob, subtract the projected PSF of that particle and re-detect in the residual image. Unlike blind peeling, use the tracked particle's known brightness/shape as the template, so order dependence is reduced. Gate: only peel when residual exceeds noise floor.

    4. Temporal de-merge via interpolation. A particle hidden in frame t by a merge can be interpolated from t-1 and t+1 (trajectory continuity). Use the interpolated position to (a) claim a sub-region of the merged blob and (b) seed a "virtual observation" for the hidden partner at reduced weight, feeding tracking.

    Theme 3 — Tracking loop changes¶

    1. Predicted-position splitting at match time. When the tracker predicts two particles near a merged blob, do not compete for the single blob; instead split the blob into two pseudo-observations at the predicted positions (with covariance inflated by merge uncertainty) and feed both. The merged blob becomes a shared measurement for two tracks (extends the existing allow_shared idea but at 3D-point level).

    2. Merged-observation flag + covariance inflation. Tag each 3D point that came from a merged blob. In the Kalman/track update, inflate its measurement noise and never use it alone to update velocity. Prevents the shifted centre from biasing trajectory and velocity (addresses corruption of partners).

    3. Bidirectional recovery pass. Run forward tracking, then a backward pass; particles missed due to merges forward may be recovered backward (and vice versa). Merge trajectories, then re-run correspondence with the recovered hidden partners as known seeds to split previously merged blobs. Iterates toward closure.

    4. Membership-driven reassignment. Use the 98 %-accurate membership inference to, post-correspondence, reassign merged blobs: if blob B is inferred to contain particles {p,q} but only p got a point, create a point for q from (a) other cameras, (b) predicted position, or (c) the merged centre split. This is the cheapest high-value change: it does not change the matcher, only adds missing points.

    Theme 4 — Global / closure¶

    1. Iterative EM over blob content. E-step: given current tracks, infer blob membership and split positions. M-step: re-run correspondence + tracking with the split observations. Repeat until membership/positions converge. The "loop that keeps track of what each blob contains" is literally this outer loop.

    2. Multi-frame data association (min-cost flow). Replace per-frame Hungarian matching with a windowed (e.g. 4-frame) assignment where a merged blob can serve multiple particles across frames with capacity constraints and trajectory-consistency costs. Expensive but directly models the one-blob-many-particles reality; the existing chunked parallel infra could host it.

    3. Deblending at segmentation. Lower the merge rate at the source: adaptive threshold + watershed deblending on touching blobs, accepting extra noise then filtering by track continuity. Reduces the 27 % to ~13 % (real wp2 level) before correspondence even sees it. Complementary, not competing.

    Suggested first experiment (cheapest, highest expected gain)¶

    Implement idea 11 (membership-driven reassignment) as a post-correspondence step: - After correspondences are built, run membership inference per blob (already ~98 %). - For each merged blob that was assigned to one point, if membership says it contains a second particle, attempt to create a point for that partner using (i) its predicted position from tracking (if a track exists) projected and matched to the merged blob's camera subset, else (ii) split the merged centre using intensity weights and triangulate with the other cameras. - Feed both points into tracking with the merged flag (idea 9). - Measure on the benchmark: vErr, yield, and whether existing (kept) particles get more accurate.

    This isolates the "hidden partner" effect the user identified as the main finding, without rewriting the matcher, and gives a measurable before/after on the same metrics used in bench/*.json.

    Investigation findings (from repo test cases / synth bench)¶

    Q1 — Does membership inference expose which particles, or just a count? - The repo stores no membership. In synth_bench, merged observations are single 3D points (the merged centre); truth identities are lost (id=-1 in proto_shared_validate). The oracle linker (_oracle) assigns each point to its single nearest truth particle, so the second constituent becomes a "missing" truth point. - Consequence: any recovery method must be benchmarked against an oracle membership first (truth particles within ~0.6 mm of a point ⇒ merged blob + its constituents). This gives the upper bound. A practical membership would use nearest predicted tracks / intensity; the existing q_seed/ghost gating already controls which new tracks may start. - Implication: Idea 11 is implementable as oracle-first to bound gain, then swap in a predictor-based membership.

    Q2 — Cost budget. - Baseline two_phase ≈ 4 s / 200 frames / ~200 particles on L1_d1. Hidden partners touch ~27 % of blob observations, i.e. tens of points per frame. A per-merged-blob recovery pass (Ideas 4/11) adds at most a few seconds (well under the 10× cost of a windowed MFA). The mixture-centroid solve (Idea 4) is the only per-point nonlinear cost; keep it optional and measure. - allow_shared=True (existing) ran in 4.3 s with zero metric change: it does not create points, so it cannot recover hidden partners — confirms recovery needs new points, not just shared links.

    Q3 — Should hidden partners start new trajectories or only attach to existing ones? - Evidence from proto_shared_validate.score_tracks: a merged row counts as 2 truths only when two tracks hold it; a single track covering it counts as 1. Starting new tracks from hidden partners risks ghost inflation, but the bench ghost metric will expose it; q_seed gating already prevents doubtful points from seeding. - Recommendation: first allow hidden partners to attach to existing tracks as shared observations (low ghost risk, measures accuracy gain on kept particles); then, only if a particle has no nearby track, seed a new track gated by q_seed. Evaluate ghost and yield to decide.

    Suggested order to check (first → last)¶

    1. Idea 11 — membership-driven reassignment, oracle membership. Cheapest; directly tests the user's main finding (hidden partners). Add missing partner points post-correspondence using oracle membership; measure vErr/yield/ghost on L1_d1/L1_d2/clustered cases. Bound on gain.
    2. Idea 9 — merged flag + covariance inflation. Tag points that came from a (recovered) merged blob; inflate measurement noise and never update velocity from them. Prevents the "partner steals merged blob as clean" corruption. Combine with (1).
    3. Idea 7 — temporal interpolation of hidden partners. For particles missing due to merges, interpolate from t-1/t+1 and seed a virtual observation. Low cost, no spatial split; complements (1) when membership is uncertain.
    4. Idea 4 — mixture-centroid solve. Fix shifted centres of kept particles once (1) shows gain. Per-merged-blob nonlinear LS; measure cost.
    5. Idea 14 — deblending at segmentation. Complementary pipeline change; reduces the 27 % merge rate toward the ~13 % real-wp2 level before correspondence. Evaluate separately.

    Defer: Idea 13 (windowed MFA) — correct but ~10× slower; only if (1)–(4) plateau. Idea 1 (blob-as-container rearchitecture) is the end state; build it after the above experiments quantify the gain.

    Open questions to settle before coding¶

    • Does membership inference expose which particles, or just a count? If count only, intensity-weighted split (idea 4) needs a separate identity source (tracking prediction).
    • What is the cost budget? Idea 13 (windowed MFA) is the "right" fix but likely 10x slower; the loop (idea 12) may be the pragmatic middle.
    • Should hidden partners be allowed to start new trajectories, or only attach to existing ones? Starting new ones risks ghost inflation; attaching only recovers within-track accuracy.
