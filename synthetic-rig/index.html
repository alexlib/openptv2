    The truth rig: an independent ray tracer for testing calibration¶

    In short

    An independent ray tracer (Snell's law in 3D) gives a test rig with a known answer. It agrees with the library's radial-shift model for flat parallel windows, and shows what tilted windows cost.

    Basics: Course page 3. Do it: Ray tracing vs radial shift.

    openptv2.synthetic_rig.TruthRig computes where a 3D point appears in each camera by tracing the light ray through the real physical layers, with Snell's law in 3D. It is deliberately not the code the library uses to calibrate and track. That gives a test bed with a known answer: the rig is the truth, a calibration is a guess about it, and every difference is known.

    Left: camera 1 and six particles in the water (syn1 geometry, glass 7.5 mm, n = 1.0 / 1.49 / 1.41). Right: zoom on the glass. The red ray is the traced path; the dotted line is the straight line to the same particle, which is what you would get without refraction. The kinks at the two surfaces are Snell's law.

    What it computes¶

    1. Layers. Per camera, the glass vector g of the calibration (|g| = distance of the water-side surface from the origin, direction = the surface normal) defines two planes: the water-side surface at |g| and the air-side surface at |g| + d. Optionally each plane's normal is rotated by a small tilt (a tilted window, or a wedge when the two differ).
    2. The ray that really arrives. For a particle P, find the unit direction u leaving the camera centre C such that after refraction at the air-glass plane (n_air → n_glass) and the glass-water plane (n_glass → n_water) the ray passes through P. This is a 2-unknown root problem per point, solved by vectorised Gauss-Newton on the perpendicular miss distance (air_direction). Vector Snell law: v = η u + (η cosθi − cosθt) n, η = n_a / n_b, total reflection gives NaN.
    3. Image. The ray, continued straight in air, defines an effective point C + u·|P − C|. That point is projected with the camera's own pinhole and lens distortion (principal point, cc, k1 k2 k3, p1 p2, affine terms) without multimedia, then converted to pixels. Points outside the sensor are NaN. observe() adds Gaussian centroid noise.
    from openptv2.synthetic_rig import TruthRig
rig = TruthRig.from_cals(cals, cpar)                     # the cameras and layers of an existing calibration
px = rig.project(points)                                 # (N, n_cams, 2) pixels
rig2 = rig.with_(n_water=1.45, tilt_back=[(0, 0.02)]*4)  # a different truth: fluid index and a wedge
rig.ray_path(0, points)                                  # (N, 4, 3): camera, air-glass point, glass-water point, particle
rig.model_cpar()                                         # the multimedia a perfect calibration would use

    The library's own model (project_pixels) does it differently: it assumes flat parallel layers and finds a radial shift factor iteratively. For flat parallel layers the two must agree. They do:

    Left: over 6000 points in the syn1 volume the tracer and the library model differ by at most 0.0003 px (median 0.00007 px): both are correct for the ideal case. Right four panels: the image shift of camera 1 when the truth deviates from the model in one way (colour = shift in px, plotted over the image).

    truth deviates from the nominal model by image shift, median / max
    fluid index + 0.04 2.07 / 3.15 px
    glass 4 mm thicker 2.98 / 3.61 px
    water-side surface tilted 1.2° 0.33 / 0.50 px
    air-side surface tilted 1.2° (wedge) 2.37 / 3.54 px

    These are large in the image, yet a calibration that is fitted afterwards (exterior, principal point, distortion) absorbs almost all of them: see docs/plans/2026-11-26-calibration-lab.md (3-36 µm left in the volume). The shift is smooth and nearly uniform across the sensor, which is exactly what camera position, cc and the principal point can imitate.

    An interactive walk-through with sliders, and a comparison with the radial-shift model, is in docs/ray-tracing-vs-radial-shift.md (marimo notebook docs/notebooks/ray_tracing_multimedia.py).

    Tests and limits¶

    tests/unit/test_synthetic_rig.py checks the agreement with the library to 2e-3 px, that each deviation moves the image by a measurable but sane amount, and that with_ copies. Reproduce the figures with uv run python bench/calibration_lab/rig_figures.py.

    Not modelled: curved or non-planar windows, layers that vary in thickness across the aperture beyond a plane tilt, light that leaves the sensor plane model (rolling shutter, vibration), dispersion, lens aberrations beyond the polynomial terms, blob shape (the detector is tested separately in bench/calibration_lab/detection_bias.py). Rays are traced from one point per camera; there is no aperture or defocus.
