    Plan: a joint stereo-matching + tracking algorithm ("joint tracker")¶

    Date: 2026-11-19. Branch: feat/zarr-only-remove-legacy. Status: step 0 measured; plan revised the same day, and again after merging feat/ilp-temporal-smoothing-impl: the current plan is docs/plans/2026-11-20-holistic-ptv.md; this file is the lab record (sections 10-16 hold the measurements). Sections 3–9 are the original design and are kept for the record; where they differ from section 11, section 11 wins. Related: docs/plans/2026-09-30-tracker-plan.md (benchmark facts), docs/plans/2026-10-09-trackcorr-two-phase-merge.md, docs/plans/unified-tracker-implementation.md (peeling, ILP, stitching).

    1. Why¶

    Problem. The baseline (trackcorr forward tracking) and every newer idea in this repo work on a 3D point cloud that the stereo matcher fixed once per frame, without any knowledge of time. Measured earlier (2026-09-30-tracker-plan.md):

    • Ghost points cause about 60 % of the remaining velocity error and nearly all wrong links. Most ghost points sit in ghost-only trajectories, often 30+ frames long.
    • Tracks rarely lose a contest (3–6 % contested, about one candidate per track). They fail because the real particle is not in the cloud at all: 12.8 % of true points are seen by ≥ 3 cameras but missing from the 3D cloud; 22 % "no candidate" at 4× density.
    • Ghost cues per point saturate (AUC about 0.92; 46 % of flagged points are real), so deleting points on a mark costs real particles. Soft, claim-based rules work.
    • Dropping whole doubtful tracks afterwards is worse than point rules, because a ghost track that formed has already taken part in the assignments.
    • Feeding track knowledge back into matching (A11) failed: the matcher gives back the same combination, because nothing can forbid one and alternatives are not kept.

    Root cause in the code. algorithms/correspondences.py::correspondences finds all 4-, 3- and 2-camera combinations that pass the epipolar band (con0), then take_best_candidates keeps a greedy non-overlapping subset by corr score (4-camera first) and throws the rest away. When a ghost combination wins, the real particles whose blobs it used become 3-camera points or vanish. Peeling and the ILP modules can only prune this greedy cloud; they never see the discarded alternatives.

    Goal. A new standalone tracker that starts from the stored 2D blobs (targets) and the calibration and decides matching and tracking together: fewer ghosts, more recovered real points, longer smooth trajectories, no wrong links.

    Decisions taken (2026-11-19). - Blobs only (target space): no image subtraction, no optical model. - New standalone tracker plugin; trackcorr, two_phase, unified stay as baselines. - Judge: scripts/synth_bench.py (truth-based) + real CompleteTest wp2 / lv_multi. - Measure the best possible gain of each ingredient first; build only what pays.

    3. The idea in plain words (original design; revised in section 11)¶

    1. Keep every candidate. Per frame, keep all blob combinations that pass the epipolar test, each with its 3D position, ray miss distance (rcm), number of cameras and brightness spread. Choose nothing yet.
    2. Tracks pick first. A track with history predicts where its particle is in the next frame. Near that prediction it takes the fitting candidate; if there is none, it projects the prediction into the cameras and takes the nearest blobs directly. Its blobs are then taken.
    3. New particles come from the leftovers. Only blobs that no track took are matched for new particles. With most blobs gone, accidental combinations (ghosts) become rare: their number grows much faster than the number of blobs.
    4. Newborns are on probation. A new candidate becomes a track only after N frames (start with 4) of smooth motion. During probation, rival newborns may use the same blob; nothing is decided. At promotion the winner takes its blobs through its whole past and the rivals that needed them are removed. A ghost made of blobs from two real particles dies here, because both real particles get promoted and take their blobs.
    5. Sharing is explicit. Two real particles can overlap in one camera. A blob may serve two confirmed tracks when both predictions fall on it (lower weight in the triangulation). So exclusivity does not delete real overlapping particles.
    6. Sweep forward, then backward, and repeat. The backward sweep extends tracks to before their birth and takes blobs there, which removes ghosts that lived early. Stop when the set of tracks no longer changes (expected 2–3 sweeps). The first frames have no tracks, so the start uses strict seeds: 4 cameras, low relative rcm, no rival candidate on the same blobs.
    7. Final cleanup over whole trajectories. Trajectory-level blob uniqueness (ILP), stitching of pieces, quality marks: all exist and are reused.

    Difference from today's peeling: it works on all candidates instead of pruning the greedy cloud; matching and linking alternate frame by frame instead of running the whole tracker several times; decisions about new points are delayed.

    Known limit. A ghost whose blobs are all false targets that are consistent across cameras (a static bright object) looks like a real stationary particle to any geometric method. It is out of scope; do not tune on speed (real data has slow real flow).

    6. Step 2: the tracker (Python prototype first)¶

    src/openptv2/joint_tracking.py (algorithm) and a thin plugin src/openptv2/plugins/joint_tracking.py, registered as joint in plugins/loader.py and tracking_registry.py. Output in the usual run-store layout (RunStore.write_correspondences, write_linkage_many, write_point_quality), so synth_bench, post-processing and export work unchanged. Angles in degrees.

    State: confirmed tracks, probation tracklets, blob ownership per frame (with a share count), the pool per frame (cached).

    Per frame, forward: 1. Confirmed tracks. Predict (track_assisted._predict_position, or temporal_smoothing.irls_predict_state for long history); gate; pick pool candidates; contested groups by layered_tracking.resolve_conflict_components (Hungarian per group). No candidate → direct claim with track_assisted._claim_particle (cameras allowed per B3, shared blobs, ray-miss and position gates). One or two missed frames are tolerated, then the track ends. 2. Probation tracklets. Same search on blobs not owned by confirmed tracks; no exclusivity among tracklets; a tracklet without continuation dies (dead-end rule). 3. Seeds. Every remaining candidate with no owned blob starts a tracklet. 4. Promotion at age N when the kink is within the data-driven tolerance (tracking_postprocess.kink_statistics, as confirm_auto). The track takes its blobs in its past frames; rivals on those blobs are removed; ties between promotable rivals go to ghost_ilp.trajectory_ilp_uniqueness for that group.

    Then the backward sweep (same code, time reversed), repeated until stable. Final: trajectory_ilp_uniqueness, stitch_tracklets_irls, positions re-triangulated from the final blob sets (lsq), ghost marks stored.

    Build order, each measured on the four cases against B0 and the bounds: 2a forward sweep (steps 1–4, no sharing) → 2b sharing → 2c backward sweep and iteration → 2d final cleanup. Revert any part that gains only by dropping true points.

    What step 0 means¶

    1. The main enemy on this benchmark is overlapping particle images (merged blobs), not ghosts stealing blobs. A merged blob has a shifted centre; a 3D point that uses it is 3–10× less accurate, and with 2–3 merged blobs it is often far enough off to count as a ghost.
    2. B2 did not measure the pool's ceiling. Its "perfect" pick preferred more cameras, so it took 4-camera combinations containing merged blobs over clean 3-camera ones. And its velocity error was averaged over a larger, noisier set of points, so it rose even where no original point changed.     4. Peeling should not be the default in unified (worse than plain trackcorr on all four cases). Separate decision for the user; not part of this plan.
    5. Not yet known: whether real data has as many merged blobs as the benchmark's merge model (synth_bench._target_rows), and whether track breaks happen at the overlap events.

    11. Revised plan (2026-11-19): merge-aware joint tracker¶

    11.1 The idea in plain words¶

    The first design said: tracks own their blobs, so ghosts lose. Step 0 shows the larger loss is elsewhere: two real particles overlap in one camera, the detector gives one blob with a shifted centre, and the matcher builds an inaccurate point or none. The matcher cannot know a blob is merged. A tracker can: two tracks that are predicted onto the same blob tell it. So:

    1. Tracks predict first. Every confirmed track predicts its 3D position and its position in each camera.
    2. Find merged blobs from the predictions. A blob is merged when two or more predictions fall on it (within the blob radius). Blob size is a second hint.
    3. Triangulate around the merge. A track uses only its clean cameras when enough remain (how many is decided by bound B3).
    4. Otherwise de-merge the centre. For a merged blob with centre c and predictions p_i, particle i gets the 2D position c + (p_i − mean(p)) (brightness-weighted mean when the brightness is known), used with a lower weight. Blobs only; no images.
    5. Keep the track through the overlap. One or two frames without a usable position are coasted, so the track does not break at the overlap.
    6. New particles come from the leftover blobs. Blobs taken by confirmed tracks are removed (a merged blob counts as taken); the existing matcher runs on the rest. This needs no change to algorithms/correspondences.py: match_frame_correspondences is called on the reduced target lists.
    7. Newborns are on probation, as in section 3 (point 4): smooth motion for N frames, dead-end rule, and at promotion the track takes its blobs through its past. The existing two_phase rules (q_seed, q_young, confirm_auto) are the starting point.
    8. Forward, then backward, until stable; final cleanup as in section 3 (points 6–7).

    The candidate pool (section 5) is no longer a required part. It comes back only if bound M1 shows a gain that the steps above cannot reach.

    13.2 What the ladder says¶

    1. The estimator and the tracker are not the limit. With exact data the whole chain is accurate to 0.0009 (L0), and two_phase links L0 almost as well as the perfect linker. Everything above that is the noise of the measurements and the association.
    2. The measurement is the bottleneck, and the measurement problem is merged blobs. Of all blob observations of particles seen by ≥ 3 cameras, 26 % (sparse) and 66 % (×4) are merged blobs (two or more particles in one blob, with a shifted centre). Using them as they are (E1b) is 2–3× worse than the greedy cloud; leaving them out (E1a) is better in accuracy but throws away 43 % (sparse) and 86 % (×4) of the particles.
    3. The ceiling of correcting merged blobs is large. If each particle in a merged blob gets its own centre to within 0.3 px (E1c): vErr 0.063 and 0.131, which is 2.9× and 3.1× lower than two_phase, while 82–85 % of the particles stay (against 68 % and 22 %), long-track completeness 0.82 and 0.84 (against 0.50 and 0.16) and about one piece per long track (against 2.3 and 1.5). This ceiling beats two_phase as long as the de-merge error stays below about 0.9 px (sparse) and 0.75 px (×4).
    4. A track can predict a blob centre well enough (B3, sparse, clean particles, linear extrapolation from 2 positions): 0.27 px RMS, median 0.21, 90 % below 0.40, 99 % below 0.62. Same at ×4: 0.28 px RMS (median 0.23, 99 % below 0.65). A quadratic extrapolation from 3 positions is worse (0.39 px sparse, 0.43 px ×4; 99 % below 1.0 px): it amplifies the noise, as in the earlier reconnect finding, so use linear prediction (or a two-sided smoothed position in a post-step). A de-merge error of 0.3–0.5 px is therefore plausible, well inside the break-even.
    5. Association (matching + linking) is the second, smaller bottleneck. Sparse: greedy cloud with a perfect linker 0.1435, two_phase 0.1825: association costs 0.039 (21 % of the error); removing every ghost from the input gains two_phase only 0.005–0.018 (section 10). Hybrid trackcorr has the best continuity but keeps 7.4 % ghosts and has the higher error.
    6. Depth noise is real but is not a lever. The depth axis carries most of the velocity error (RMS 26 mm/s against 4–5 mm/s in-plane for clean particles, sparse, 150 particles). Fitting one trajectory directly to all blob centres (image space, m3) instead of fitting 3D points improves it by only 5 % (0.0501 → 0.0476), 8 % at ×4 (0.0668 → 0.0615) and 3 % with merged blobs (0.277 → 0.269). Merged blobs make the same particles 5.5× worse (0.050 → 0.277). So the per-frame triangulation + polynomial smoothing is already close to the information in the blobs; improving the blobs is what pays. (The ×4 run with merged blobs gave no result.)
    7. Speed. The matcher costs 0.10 s per frame (sparse, 1150 points) and 0.44 s per frame (×4, 2560 points); two_phase links at 0.02–0.03 s per frame, hybrid trackcorr 0.19, merged unified 0.5–1.0. So the matcher is the cost of the fast pipeline. Any scheme that runs the matcher more than once per frame, or builds a large candidate pool, multiplies the dominant cost. De-merging is cheap (a projection and a small solve per merged blob).

    13.3 Levers that the measurements rule out (do not spend time here)¶

    • A candidate pool / perfect selection from it (B1, B2): recovers part of the lost points, but the recovered ones are the merged-blob points and they are noisy; the same points are better served by correcting the blob.
    • Image-space trajectory fitting as such (3–8 %).
    • Ghost removal on two_phase (0.005–0.018). (Ghosts do matter for hybrid trackcorr: 7.4 %.)
    • Cost weights, wider gates, re-matching freed blobs (earlier findings, 2026-09-30-tracker-plan.md).
    • trajectory_stitch.stitch_trajectories in its present form (section 12).

    13.4 Caveats (read before trusting the ceiling)¶

    • The merge model is the benchmark's (synth_bench._target_rows, calibrated to reproduce the image pipeline's 2D recall to ~2 %); whether real recordings have as many merged blobs is not measured (bound M0). Everything in 13.2 points 2–3 depends on it.
    • E1c assumes the particles in each merged blob are known, and that each gets a centre with independent error σ. A real implementation must find the particles from tracks, which exist only after the particle was tracked before or after the merge (cold start, long overlaps), and must split the blob between them using brightness. The ceiling is for the case where this works.
    • B3 uses clean particles with perfectly associated history; real histories contain errors.

    14. Current plan (2026-11-19, after the bottleneck analysis): merge-aware joint tracker¶

    Replaces sections 11.1–11.3 where they differ; 11.5 (scoring) and 12 (merged branch, baselines) still apply.

    14.1 Principle (no fixed order)¶

    The information must flow both ways. Detections → matcher → 3D points → tracks is the old direction. The new one: tracks → which blobs are merged and where each particle sits inside them → better 3D points and particles the matcher could not form → better tracks. It is one loop, started from the existing matcher and linker, iterated until stable, and it can run forward in time, backward, or both. The matcher still runs once per frame (it is the cost); no candidate pool.

    14.2 The loop¶

    1. Start: greedy cloud from the existing matcher; link with the better of two_phase and hybrid trackcorr (decided by J0 below). These tracks are the first predictions.
    2. Track-informed measurement correction (J1). For every track point and every camera, project the track's predicted position (two-sided smoothed position when run as a post-step: its error is smaller than the causal prediction's 0.27 px). Several predictions (of different particles) on one blob, or a blob much brighter or larger than its track's usual (brightness is persistent per particle and camera: linked pairs change it by a median 0.06), mark the blob as merged. The blob has one measured centre c, the brightness-weighted mean of the spots' centres s_j = p_j + δ_j (p_j predicted). The minimum-norm solution of "weighted mean of s_j equals c" is δ_j = w_j r / Σ w_k² with r = c − Σ w_j p_j and w_j the brightness shares. Each particle gets the pixel s_j, with a larger variance than a clean blob. Then re-triangulate (least squares, per-camera weights: track_assisted._triangulate_weighted_lsq); when ≥ 2 clean cameras remain and the merged ones disagree, drop the merged ones.
    3. Recovery (J2). A particle whose blob was merged is often missing from the cloud (the matcher could not form it). Tracks that end or have a gap, and whose prediction falls on a merged blob, take the blob with the same split (a claim in blob space, track_assisted._claim_particle with shared blobs), add the point and continue. New particles in merged blobs have no track: they enter from the leftover blobs after the tracks have taken theirs (existing matcher on the reduced target lists), on probation (smoothness for N frames, dead-end rule, taking blobs through the past at promotion).
    4. Iterate (J3). Better positions give better predictions and better links; repeat 2–3 with a forward and a backward sweep until the set of tracks is stable (expected 2–3 rounds).
    5. Final cleanup with what exists and has been measured to help: reconnect.reconnect_pieces, smoothness_filter, quality_post trimming, IRLS stitching. Not stitch_trajectories (section 12).

    15. Reframing (2026-11-19): one space–time–camera inference, not a pipeline¶

    15.1 The assumption that was wrong¶

    Detection → stereo matching → tracking assumes every blob is one particle, every stage is decided once, and each stage only passes its result on. Section 13 showed that the first assumption fails for 26 % (sparse) to 66 % (×4) of the blob observations, and that this, not the linker, limits the accuracy. The deeper point: the image plane, the 3D position and time are one object. A blob is evidence about particles that exist in 3D and move through time; and what a blob contains is visible only from its context: - in time: particles enter and leave a blob; a particle that was alone in the blob before and after is inside it now; - across cameras: a 3D particle seen cleanly by 2–3 cameras predicts where it must be in the others; a blob that is larger/brighter or off-centre there is a merged one; - in appearance: each particle has a persistent brightness per camera; a blob's grey sum is (about) the sum of its particles' brightnesses, so the count is readable. A single frame knows none of this; it can only learn "what a typical particle image looks like".

    15.3 The model¶

    Latent: particles k with smooth 3D trajectories X_k(t) and per-camera brightness a_{k,c} (persistent, slowly varying). Observations: per camera and frame, blobs with centre, pixel count, extent and grey sum. Blob model: a blob holds m ≥ 1 spots; its centre is the brightness-weighted mean of the spots' centres, its grey sum the sum of their brightnesses, its size grows with m and the spots' separation. Detection → matching → tracking is a greedy coordinate ascent on this model with the wrong m = 1. The better procedure is an order-free loop (EM / coordinate ascent): 1. Explain blob mass. For each blob: the tracked particles that project into it and their brightness a_{k,c} explain a part of its grey sum and shift its centre; the split of the centre uses the minimum-norm rule of section 14.2 point 2. 2. Update trajectories and brightness from the de-merged measurements (re-triangulation with per-camera weights; clean cameras dominate). 3. Look at the residual. Grey sum not explained by the tracked particles in a blob, in a camera where the 3D evidence says there should be exactly one more particle (or two cameras see a clean particle that is absent here), is a birth hypothesis; a track whose blob is empty or too weak is an occlusion (coast) or a death. Births and deaths are what enter/leave events are. 4. Repeat in any order, forward and backward in time, until stable. The matcher keeps its role as a cheap generator of first hypotheses (once per frame), not as the authority.

    15.4 What changes in the plan (section 14)¶

    • J1 becomes the blob-content model, not just a re-triangulation: (a) per track and camera a brightness signature a_{k,c} (median over alone-in-blob frames, updated); (b) mass accounting to count the members of a blob and to flag merged blobs (the 0.95 AUC cue) instead of using only "two predictions on one blob"; (c) the minimum-norm centre split; (d) camera selection by the cross-camera evidence (use clean cameras when ≥ 2 exist: 64 % of the merged observations at sparse density).
    • J2 (recovery) is the birth/death step of 15.3: unexplained blob mass + cross-camera evidence create a particle; the matcher's leftovers are one source of hypotheses.
    • A new first step, J1-oracle: run the J1 algorithm on perfectly associated data (the ladder inputs: truth identities, merged blobs left as they are, E1b) and compare with the ceilings E1c (0.063 sparse, 0.131 ×4) and E1b (0.230 / 0.868). It measures how much of the ceiling the estimation captures without the association problem. Then replace truth identities by tracks (two_phase, hybrid trackcorr) and measure the loss of association (J1 proper).
    • ×4 needs joint hypotheses over clusters (more than half of merged observations lack a clean 3D moment within ± 6 frames). Plan it as stage J3b after J1–J3 are measured, not before.
    • M0 stays the gate (real merge rate), now with a second part: the stability of real blob brightness per particle (the appearance cue) on wp2 tracks: median |Δ log sumg| of linked pairs and its 95th percentile.
