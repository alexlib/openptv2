    Holistic 3D-PTV: one inference over cameras, space and time¶

    Date: 2026-11-20. Branch: feat/zarr-only-remove-legacy. Status: approved; implementation follows the stages below (progress log at the end). The measurements it rests on are in docs/plans/2026-11-19-joint-tracker.md, sections 10-16 (lab record: bounds, error-budget ladder, merged-branch baselines, cue measurements, M0 gate). Scripts: scripts/joint_bounds.py, joint_info_coverage.py, joint_blob_context.py, joint_blob_shape.py, joint_m0.py, synth_bench.py (MERGE_SCALE). Numbers: bench/step23_joint_bounds_2026-11.json. Work in the worktree /Users/alex/Documents/Github/openptv2-joint (own environment, Python 3.13, Cython built).

    Context¶

    openptv2 runs detection → stereo matching → tracking, each decided once, each blind to the others. We measured where that loses (truth benchmark, 2026-11-19):

    • The chain itself is fine: with exact data it is accurate to 0.0009 (velocity error).
    • The limit is the measurement, and the measurement problem is merged blobs: two or more particles in one detected blob with a shifted centre. 27 % of blob observations on the benchmark, 66 % at 4× density; on real wp2 roughly half to equal the benchmark's rate.
    • Ceiling if each particle in a merged blob gets its own centre to 0.3 px: velocity error 0.063 against 0.183 for the best tracker today (two_phase), 82 % of particles kept against 68 %, long-track completeness 0.82 against 0.50. At real-like merge rates still 2× lower error, +4–7 points of particles, +8–12 points of completeness.
    • The information to do it exists, but only jointly: per-camera history resolves 31 % of merged observations, other cameras 64 %, cameras + nearby frames in 3D 76 %. A blob's brightness against its particle's own typical brightness tells merged from single with AUC 0.95 (weaker on real data); shape change is a weak cue; a track predicts a blob centre to 0.27 px (linear; quadratic is worse).
    • Association is the other half at real-like merge rates, and 11 % of true particles have no blob at all (too faint): the yield ceiling in blob space is about 0.82–0.86.
    • Ruled out: candidate pool, image-space trajectory fit as such (3–8 %), ghost removal for two_phase, cost weights, stitch_trajectories (harmful), peeling as default.

    Goal: a breakthrough against the sequential baselines, not an increment: one procedure in which detection evidence, stereo geometry and motion resolve each other's ambiguities. Decision already taken: blob attributes first; pixel patches only if the split of merged centres is shown to be the shortfall.

    The idea in plain words¶

    Stop asking "which blobs match?" and then "which points link?". Ask one question: which set of moving particles explains every blob in every camera in every frame?

    • A particle is a smooth 3D path plus a brightness in each camera (stable in time).
    • A blob is explained by the particles that project into it: its brightness is the sum of theirs, its centre their brightness-weighted mean.
    • Everything is scored in one currency (a cost):
    • distance between each blob centre and what its particles predict;
    • brightness of each blob against the sum of its particles' brightness;
    • smoothness of each path (acceleration);
    • a price for a blob nobody explains (false target), and a price for a particle that should be visible in a camera but has no blob (cheap when the particle is faint).
    • Matching, linking, counting particles in a blob, starting and ending tracks are all the same kind of move: accept it if the total cost goes down. A ghost dies by itself: it claims brightness that real particles already account for. An overlap is not a failure: both particles keep their paths and share the blob.
    • Order is free: the existing matcher and tracker give the first guess; then the loop runs forward and backward in time until nothing improves.

    This is the Shake-the-Box principle (predict, then resolve the image) moved to blob space with brightness accounting, so it stays fast and works without an optical model.

    Stages (each measured; stop rules are part of the plan)¶

    Benchmark cases for every stage: sparse, ×2, ×4 at the default merge rate and sparse at real-like rates (MERGE_SCALE=0.75, 0.6; build 150–200-frame versions). Baselines: two_phase, hybrid trackcorr, unified+stitch=false. Score on the common set of points (below) plus yield, long-track completeness, pieces per long track, ghost share.

    H0. Foundations (small, first) - Observation table: per 3D point and camera the pixel position actually used, its variance and the number of particles sharing the blob. New optional arrays in RunStore (storage/run_store.py), written next to correspondences/. Consumers: link confirmation and ghost marks use the per-point variance (below), point_quality.rcm_from_arrays the corrected pixels. Reviewed assumption (2026-11-20): the first guess was that later steps recompute ray miss from the uncorrected blob centres, so corrected points look like ghosts. Measured: switching the ghost rules off changes two_phase on the corrected points from 0.138 to 0.141 (no effect); switching off its link confirmation as well gives 0.076 (perfect linker 0.060). So the cause is the confirmation tolerance (8x the median kink of the run), which cuts true links of the noisier corrected points. Fix to build in H2: confirmation tolerance per link from the points' own variances instead of one run-wide value. - Scoring in scripts/synth_bench.py::evaluate_pred: subset (truth keys matched by plain two_phase) → velocity and position error on the common set and on the extra points separately. - Truth-free score for real data: leave-one-camera-out. Reconstruct each particle from three cameras, project into the fourth, compare with the blob there (scripts/joint_m0.py grows this). Works on real wp2 and on the benchmark, so the two can be compared directly.

    H1. The blob model with perfect association (isolates the estimation) New module src/openptv2/blob_model.py; driver in scripts/joint_bounds.py. Input: truth identities, merged blobs as detected (the E1b input). Implement: - brightness signature per particle and camera (median grey sum over frames where it is alone in its blob; updated in the loop); - membership and count per blob from projected paths and brightness accounting; - centre split: for blob centre c, predictions p_j, brightness shares w_j: δ_j = w_j·r / Σw_k², r = c − Σ w_j p_j; each particle gets p_j + δ_j with a larger variance than a clean blob; - camera choice: clean cameras only when ≥ 2 exist, else weighted (track_assisted._triangulate_weighted_lsq); - path refinement and repeat (2–3 rounds). Gate: closes at least 60 % of the gap between "merged blobs as they are" and the ceiling (sparse 0.230 → 0.063, ×4 0.868 → 0.131; real-like 0.106 → 0.060). If it falls short, compare the centre error after the split with the 0.27 px prediction error: if equal, the split is at its limit and the patch option opens (user decision of 2026-11-19); if not, fix the model.

    H2. The same on real tracks, as a post-step (no link changes) merge_aware_refine(store, linkage_name) on finished two_phase and hybrid trackcorr runs; pick the better linker as the core for H3. Gate: common-set velocity error −30 % at the default merge rate and −20 % at MERGE_SCALE=0.75; under −15 %: stop and find where association loses it.

    H3. Births, deaths and links by the same cost - Through an overlap: a track whose prediction lands on a shared blob keeps going (track_assisted._claim_particle with shared blobs), instead of ending. - Cross-camera completion: a particle seen cleanly by 2–3 cameras takes its share of the blob in the remaining camera. - Births from unexplained brightness and from leftover blobs (existing match_frame_correspondences on the reduced target lists; no change to algorithms/correspondences.py); newborns on probation (smooth for N frames, dead-end rule). - Links: join two pieces when the joint path lowers the cost (reconnect.reconnect_pieces as the candidate generator; not trajectory_stitch.stitch_trajectories). Gate: yield +10 points at the default rate and +5 at 0.75; extra points' position error below 2× the common set's; ghost share not higher.

    H4. The full loop Sliding window in time, forward and backward, all moves under one cost, until stable. For dense data (more than half of merged observations have no clean 3D moment within ±6 frames at 4×): hypotheses over a whole cluster of overlapping particles and frames (small exact search per cluster, ghost_ilp.solve_component_ilp style). Plugin holistic (plugins/loader.py, tracking_registry.py); hot loops to Cython only now.

    H5. Proof on images and real data - Rendered images, not only synthetic blob tables: the native-density render exists (Synthetic-CompleteTest/syn1); render ×2 with openptv-analysis synthetic_experiment for a dense image-level case. - Real wp2 (300 frames, then all 1072) and lv_multi on scratch copies: leave-one-camera-out error, tracks ≥ 50 frames, roughness, and the Eulerian statistics against the MATLAB reference (comparison_vs_legacy.json).

    Open points¶

    • unified defaults (stitch on, peeling on) hurt on the truth benchmark; changing them is a separate decision for the user.
    • The 11 % of particles with no blob need the images (track-guided detection with a lower local threshold). Outside this plan unless the user opens the image option.
    • Real-data brightness is less stable than synthetic (linked pairs: 95 % within ×1.3 against ×1.16), so thresholds come from each run's own spread.

    Synthesis (2026-11-20): what the measurements say the best tracker is¶

    The essential difficulty is not linking and not ghosts. It is that a blob is not a particle. With exact data the chain is accurate to 0.0009; the linkers find 99 % of the reachable links; removing every ghost gains two_phase 0.5-2 %. What limits everything is that 27 % of blob observations (benchmark default; roughly half that on real wp2) hold several particles. The matcher, forced to give each blob to one point, then (a) builds the visible particle from a shifted centre and (b) leaves the other particle out of the cloud; the left-out particle in turn corrupts its partner, which takes the merged blob as its own. The biggest single finding of this work: the loss is hidden partners, and bringing them back makes the particles we already had more accurate.

    What each existing piece contributes (measured on the truth benchmark): | piece | where it lives | effect | keep? | |---|---|---|---| | greedy epipolar matcher | algorithms/correspondences.py | good first guess, fast (0.1 s/frame) | yes, as the seed | | two_phase linker + ghost marks (q_seed, q_young) | plugins/two_phase_tracking.py | best linker; marks give 0.5-2 % | yes, as the linking core | | two_phase confirmation (confirm_auto) | same | cuts true links of noisier points | yes, but per-point tolerance from variances | | hybrid trackcorr (3D cascade + epipolar) | plugins/default_tracking.py | best continuity, keeps 2x the ghosts | alternative core, not default | | peeling (Clean-and-Claim) | layered_tracking.py | worse than plain trackcorr everywhere | no (its idea is done better by membership + births) | | trajectory ILP, IRLS stitching | ghost_ilp.py, temporal_smoothing.py | no measured gain | optional | | stitch_trajectories | trajectory_stitch.py | 3x worse velocity error | no | | blob model: membership, effective observations, trusted LOO path prior | blob_model.py, merge_refine.py, merge_post.py | -14 % (common set) | yes | | hidden-partner mass check, long-gap recovery with mass check | same | -25 % cumulative | yes | | births from residual brightness, relink, 2 rounds | same + joint_bounds.py h3 | -43 % cumulative; +5 points yield; +13 points long completeness | yes: the core of the new method |

    Recommended architecture ("holistic" tracker = the order-free loop): 1. detect -> greedy matcher -> two_phase (seed tracks); 2. measurement layer: infer shared blobs from tracks, effective observations, weighted fit with the trusted path prior; 3. mass accounting per blob: hidden partners -> residual targets -> births (matcher on residual + free targets); 4. relink on the corrected, augmented cloud; reconnect pieces; recover long gaps (Hermite path + mass check); 5. repeat 2-4 (two rounds measured, still improving); 6. output with a variance per point (the observation table), used by weighted smoothing and by the user. unified should become this loop on top of its existing infrastructure (estimator, TrackingDecision, probes), with peeling and stitch off.

    Open, in order of value: 1. Real data: none of the loop has run on wp2 yet. Use leave-one-camera-out consistency, tracks >= 50 frames, roughness, and the Eulerian statistics against the MATLAB reference. Merge rate there is about half, so expect a smaller but real gain (the real-like benchmark cases predicted -27 % for perfect association). 2. The new points are noisy (velocity error 0.27-0.30, 6-7 % of the output): use their variance in smoothing, and noise-aware confirmation, before counting them as yield. 3. Births recall: ~58/frame against ~250 missed; residual placement degrades for dim hidden particles. 4. Dense data (x4): cluster hypotheses (H4); the blob model alone closes only 39 % there. 5. Speed: the loop is python (~0.6 s/frame per round against 0.1 matcher + 0.02 linker); vectorise / Cython after 1-2.

    Signatures, safe births, joint split, dense matcher (2026-11-20, third part)¶

    Signature estimation (merge_post.estimate_signatures, tests): start from a low quantile, then alternate (observations whose blob grey sum is 0.75-1.3x the summed signatures of its tracked members are explained; each member's share of an explained blob gives its signature) and finally a cross-camera step (brightness per camera = camera gain x particle brightness; a camera whose signature is missing or > 1.35x what the other cameras predict takes the cross-camera value, since a partner may hide in that camera's blob in every frame). Births recall (scripts/joint_birth_recall.py, L1 default): good residual targets 13.6 % (median) -> 15.9 % (joint only) -> 32.1 % (joint + cross-camera) of hidden views; unknown signatures 10.4 % -> 0 %; missed points with >= 3 usable views 26.7 % -> 46.8 %.

    Safe births as members (refine_merged(partner_min_len=10)): a member whose track has < 10 rows does not lend its prediction to its partners (counts as unknown for them), so young and wrong births cannot corrupt established particles. refine_merged(signatures=...) takes the joint signatures.

    Image-pipeline case (L2), two rounds, joint signatures + safe births (joint_bounds.py h3 --joint-sig --partner-min-len 10): velocity error 0.1195 (two_phase 0.1400, -15 %), common set 0.0776 (0.1223, -37 %), yield 0.745 (0.691), long completeness 0.653 (0.544), pieces per long track 1.57 (2.03), ghost 3.5 % (3.7 %). Before this change the same loop gave 0.1298 / 0.0938 / 0.726 / 0.614. A final refinement after the last relink still hurts the overall error (0.2273): the pipeline ends at the relink.

    Joint cluster split (from the idea "membership + mixture-centroid split + flag"; blob_model.solve_cluster, refine_merged(mode="joint"), tests): every cluster of points sharing blobs in a frame is solved jointly by Gauss-Newton: clean blobs give each member's own equations, a shared blob one equation (centre = brightness-weighted mean of the members' projections), trusted path predictions pseudo-observations; no member relies on a partner's prediction. Python, slow (one small solve per cluster). Comparison with the per-member split under true association: see the next entry.

    Dense-aware matcher, first prototype (joint_bounds.py mm, mass_select, tests): candidate pool selected greedily with blob capacities from brightness (grey sum / typical single grey sum of the camera's smaller blobs, <= 3) and at most one shared blob between two taken points. x4: points per frame 2,597 -> 4,428, two_phase yield 0.227 -> 0.242, ghost 6.8 % -> 13.8 %, common set 0.390 -> 0.357; default rate: yield 0.684 -> 0.691, ghost 3.9 % -> 5.3 %, common set unchanged. Negative: brightness capacity within one frame mostly creates ghosts. Capacity must be granted only where tracks back it (the births mechanism does that), i.e. the dense matcher has to be the births loop with cluster-level hypotheses in time, not a single-frame matcher.

    Joint cluster split, first comparison (true association, L1 default, 60 frames): diverges. Median error is fine (0.041 mm against 0.032 for the per-member split), but the RMS explodes over the rounds (round 1 6e6 mm): clusters in which a member is constrained only through shared blobs (or one clean view) are underdetermined, the undamped Gauss-Newton step runs away, and the runaway positions then poison the path predictions (learned prediction variance 65,000 px^2). Open bug, not usable yet. To fix next: Levenberg-Marquardt damping with a step limit, always include the member's previous position (or trusted path) as a weak prior, accept a cluster solution only if its reprojection cost drops and positions stay within a few blob radii of the start, else keep the per-member result. The x4 run of it was stopped (slow, 15 GB). Per-member split (mode="effective") stays the default.

    State at the end of this session. Best pipeline (benchmark-verified): two_phase -> rounds of [extend membership -> joint + cross-camera signatures -> refine (effective split, trusted LOO prior, hidden-partner check, young members not lent to partners) -> births from residual brightness -> relink with two_phase], output = the last relink. L2 image-pipeline case: velocity error -15 %, common set -37 %, yield +5.4 points, long completeness +11 points, ghost unchanged. Real wp2 (older version of the loop): tracks 43 % longer, 21 % more tracks >= 50 frames, better cross-camera consistency of corrected blobs. Not solved: dense (x4); speed (python, about 0.6 s/frame per round). Next: fix the joint solve (damping), rerun wp2 with the current loop, a births loop with cluster hypotheses for dense data, then the holistic plugin.
