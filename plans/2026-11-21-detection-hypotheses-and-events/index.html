    Detection that keeps its doubts: blob hypotheses for overlapping tracers¶

    Date: 2026-11-21. Follows 2026-11-21-what-we-missed.md (M1 becomes stage D1 here) and 2026-11-21-holistic-next-phase.md (the holistic loop becomes one of the users of the hypotheses).

    Scope. Tracers only. The goal is a 3D-PTV tracker (not Shake-The-Box) that is clearly better than the older libptv trackcorr-based solution. Colliding or breaking particles are only a picture that helps think about overlaps; overlapping tracers do not behave like them. They pass each other and keep their own paths.

    0. Where we stand, and the main point¶

    • We are sparse. Real wp2 has 0.0057 particles per pixel (ppp) and lv-multi wp4 has 0.0053 ppp, counting detected blobs only. The true density is about 0.007 ppp, since merged and missed particles are not counted. Image-based tracking (Shake-The-Box and similar) runs at 0.05-0.1 ppp.
    • Even so, centroids lose 7-15 % of particles to overlap. Random placement at 0.0057 ppp puts a neighbour within 2-3 px of 1 - exp(-ρπd²) = 7-15 % of particles, which matches the roughly 13 % merged rate on wp2. This comes from geometry, not detector settings, and it grows quickly with seeding (50-75 % at 0.05 ppp).
    • Our synthetic benchmark is denser than the real data (27 % merged against about 13 %), so it overweights overlaps, and gains measured on it shrink on real data.
    • At real density, overlap is not the only loss. About 11-15 % of true particles have no blob (too faint), and the calibration floor sets the position noise.
    • The main point. Detection makes one hard decision per blob ("one particle, this centre") and throws the image away. Overlapped tracers and faint tracers are both detection doubts that were silently resolved the wrong way, and no later step can undo that.

    1. Goal and principles¶

    1. Better than trackcorr, measured. Every stage is scored against legacy trackcorr (and our current best, two_phase with holistic rounds) on the same cases and metrics.
    2. Quality first, then density. First raise track quality at today's density (about 0.005 ppp). Then raise the seeding step by step (0.005 -> 0.01 -> 0.02 -> 0.04 ppp, synthetic first) and find where each part breaks.
    3. Learn from image-space methods; do not rebuild Shake-The-Box. We take a measured per-camera particle image shape, the idea that a blob is explained by the tracers inside it, and the use of track predictions to resolve the image. We do not take the global loop that re-renders and shakes every particle in every frame.
    4. Keep the doubt, resolve it lazily. Detection writes its best answer exactly as today, plus the alternatives it was unsure about, each with a probability. Later steps use an alternative only where they need one.
    5. Only detection, stereo and time together decide. Whether a blob is one tracer or two overlapping tracers is often undecidable in one image. Other cameras (where the two are apart) and the tracks before and after (two smooth paths entering and leaving) decide it.

    2. Detection that keeps its doubts¶

    2.1 What detection writes¶

    For each blob, the primary target exactly as today, plus a small hypothesis record:

    Field Meaning
    p_single probability that the blob holds one tracer
    k2: (x1, y1, x2, y2, share, p) best two-tracer explanation: two centres, the grey share of the first, its probability
    k3: ..., p three-tracer explanation, only when k2 fits clearly better than one tracer
    shape: (sxx, sxy, syy) grey-weighted second moments (size and elongation)
    brightness mean intensity, sumg / n

    Plus a list of faint candidates: local maxima above a lower threshold (for example half of gvthres) that did not become targets, with centre, brightness and a probability.

    The primary target list does not change, so matching and tracking see the same input as today. The hypotheses cost nothing unless a later step uses them.

    2.2 Brightness and size¶

    • Brightness is mean intensity, sumg / n. For one tracer it is a property of the tracer and its distance from the light sheet, so it changes slowly along a track and works as the tracer's signature in each camera.
    • Overlap shows mainly in size and shape. Two overlapping tracer images give a blob with more pixels and an elongated shape, while its mean intensity stays close to that of a single tracer (a little higher where the two images add up).
    • The centre split needs the grey shares. A blob's centre is the grey-weighted mean of the tracers inside it, so splitting it uses each tracer's share of the summed grey value (its mean intensity times its area), not the mean intensity alone.
    • Today the holistic loop uses the summed grey value (sumg) as signature (holistic.estimate_signatures). D1 measures which cue separates one tracer from two best on truth: mean intensity, pixel count, the second moments, or the summed value. The signature follows the result.

    2.3 How it is computed, fast¶

    All of this comes from the region-growing loop in segmentation._vectorized_targ_rec, which already visits every pixel of every blob once:

    1. Second moments in the same loop. Add grey-weighted sums of x², xy and y² next to the existing weighted sums: three more additions per pixel.
    2. A per-camera particle image size from the data. Take isolated, unsaturated blobs (no neighbour within 6 px) and record their second moments. Their median is the camera's single-tracer size σ²_psf, and their spread tells how much a single tracer varies. This is the image-space point-spread-function idea, done with numbers we already have.
    3. Split by moments (closed form). Two tracers at distance d with grey shares w₁ and w₂ add w₁w₂d² to the variance along the line joining them. So the excess along the major axis, λ₁ - σ²_psf, gives the separation, and the third moment along that axis gives the share. That yields k2 with no iteration. A short two-Gaussian fit on the blob's own pixels (10-30 pixels) refines it only for blobs kept as doubtful.
    4. Peaks inside the blob. Today the region growing absorbs a second local maximum when the dip between them is smaller than discont. We record that maximum instead of losing it. Two maxima are strong evidence for k2. A merged blob with a single maximum is the hard case that the moments handle.
    5. Probabilities. A small calibrated model (logistic regression on excess variance over σ²_psf, eccentricity, number of internal maxima, mean intensity against the tracer population, pixel count), trained on syn1, where truth tells how many tracers each blob holds. On real data σ²_psf and the brightness scale come from the run.

    Cost: a few more operations per pixel in a loop that runs anyway, plus a fit for the doubtful 10-25 % of blobs.

    2.4 Where it is stored¶

    New optional arrays in RunStore next to targets/: target_hyp/cam_{c}/{frame} (one row per blob, keyed by the target's pnr) and faint/cam_{c}/{frame}. Old stores without them work as now. Readers and writers sit beside write_targets / read_targets in storage/run_store.py.

    3. Using the doubts where they matter (conflict sites)¶

    An alternative is consulted only at a conflict site. These are a few percent of all observations, which keeps the method fast.

    Conflict site What triggers it What the alternative gives
    Stereo: a camera is missing a 3D candidate seen in 2-3 cameras projects, in the remaining camera, onto a blob already used, or onto nothing a k2 centre on the epipolar line inside the used blob, or a faint candidate there; the point gets that camera with a larger uncertainty
    Tracking: a prediction lands in a taken blob two tracks predict into the same blob the k2 pair, assigned to the two tracks by their predictions; both tracks continue with their own centres instead of one ending
    Tracking: a prediction lands on nothing a good track has no blob within the search radius a faint candidate near the prediction; the gap is filled instead of the track ending
    Identity swap a track zig-zags (a jump and a jump back, or acceleration far above its own level) next to another track the k2 record of the blobs along the jump; the two tracks are re-assigned so both become smooth
    Overlap window tracks end, break or swap where they meet a shared blob the overlap-window solver of section 4

    The first two are what the holistic loop already does by inference from predictions (docs/holistic-tracking.md). The hypotheses give it measured sub-blob centres to check against, instead of positions inferred from the tracks only.

    4. Resolving an overlap window¶

    4.1 What can explain a shared blob¶

    Explanation In 3D What the cameras see
    Overlap (the common case) two tracers pass each other at a distance larger than their image size a merged blob in one or two cameras, at frames that differ between cameras; separate blobs in the others; afterwards both continue on their own smooth paths
    Split image of one tracer one tracer its blob breaks into two at the threshold in some frames or cameras only (noise, uneven illumination)
    Ghost no tracer a stereo candidate that borrows a blob already explained by real tracers

    4.2 What tells them apart¶

    • Other cameras. Where the two tracers are still apart, they give two 3D points whose distance stays larger than the image size. That settles "overlap" in most cases (cameras plus nearby frames resolved 76 % of merged observations on the benchmark, 2026-11-19-joint-tracker.md).
    • Frames differ between cameras. An overlap depends on the viewing direction, so it starts and ends at different frames in different cameras. A blob that is merged in every camera at once needs the tracks to decide.
    • Duration. An overlap lasts about blob size divided by relative image speed: a few frames.
    • Tracks before and after. Two smooth paths enter the shared blob and two leave it. Following them forward and backward fixes the first and last shared frames in each camera.
    • Size and shape. In the overlap camera the blob is larger and elongated along the line between the predicted positions; its mean intensity stays near a single tracer's.

    4.3 The solver¶

    For each window (2-3 tracks, about 20 frames, all cameras): fit both tracers' 3D paths through the window, with the observation model of the holistic loop (a blob is explained by the tracers projecting into it, its centre is their grey-weighted mean) and the k2 centres as extra measurements. Compare with "one tracer" and "one of the two is a ghost", with a penalty for each extra tracer. Keep the best, write its probability, and pass the two continuous tracks on.

    This is a small exact problem, solved only at the few windows where tracks meet a shared blob.

    5. What we take from image-space methods, and what we leave¶

    Idea Image-space methods (Shake-The-Box, IPR) Here
    Particle image shape optical transfer function calibrated per camera and volume region per-camera σ²_psf from isolated blobs, per run
    Overlapping tracers fit the image of all particles jointly, every frame split only doubtful blobs, from moments plus a small fit; used only at conflict sites
    Track-guided detection predicted particles shaken in the image; residual image searched for new ones the prediction asks for a stored alternative (sub-blob centre or faint candidate); no residual images
    Ghost removal ghosts fade because the image does not support their intensity blob accounting in blob space (holistic loop)

    The pixels are read once, at detection, in the loop that already runs. A later stage (D6) may re-read small windows around predictions for faint tracers, but there is never a global image loop.

    6. Related methods to read before building (from memory; check the papers)¶

    • u-track (Jaqaman et al., Nature Methods 2008): 2D tracking with explicit merge and split events and an intensity cost; a tested formulation of tracks meeting in one blob.
    • Conservation tracking (Schiegg et al., ICCV 2013; ilastik): a detection may hold several objects (a count variable), chosen jointly with tracking; once the count is known, the blob is split by fitting that many Gaussians. That is "keep the doubt, resolve it later" in published form.
    • Segmentation hypotheses (Jug et al. 2014; Arteta et al., MICCAI 2012): a tree of blobs at several thresholds, from which tracking picks a consistent set. Our internal-maxima record is a small version of this tree.
    • Shake-The-Box / IPR (Schanz et al., Wieneke) and OpenLPT (open source): comparators at the density ladder, and their point-spread-function practice.

    7. Stages, each measured; stop rules are part of the plan¶

    Scoring as in 2026-11-21-what-we-missed.md: one scorecard (scripts/synth_bench.py eval, bench/*.json), the same cases for every claim. Baselines in every table: legacy trackcorr and two_phase with holistic rounds. A stage that gains only by dropping true points is reverted.

    D0. Truth cases and baselines (first; about a day) - Re-render syn1 at the real merged rate (about 13 %) next to the current 27 %, so gains are measured where the real data is. - Density ladder for later: 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.04 ppp. - Per-camera σ²_psf on syn1 and on wp2 (scratch copy): does the real particle image behave like the rendered one? - Legacy trackcorr scored on all cases, so "better than trackcorr" has numbers.

    D1. The hypothesis detector, scored against truth (replaces M1) - Second moments, internal maxima, moment split, faint candidates, calibrated probabilities (section 2), and the comparison of cues (mean intensity, pixel count, moments, summed grey value). - Score per blob against syn1/truth/truth.npz: k2 centre error against the true centres; recall and false rate of faint candidates; calibration of p_single. - Compare with the detectors on feat/detection-prototypes-dask-trackpy-skimage-proptv on the same scorecard. - Gate: k2 centres within 0.5 px of truth for at least half of the two-tracer blobs; p_single calibrated within 0.1; faint candidates recover at least a third of the tracers with no blob, with fewer than 2 false candidates per true one. If the k2 error is no better than the 0.27 px track prediction, the split adds nothing over inference, and we keep only the faint candidates.

    D2. Stereo and tracking use the alternatives (conflict sites 1-3) - Missing-camera completion in matching; shared-blob claims and faint-candidate gap filling in two_phase and the holistic loop. - Gate (benchmark at the real merged rate): yield +5 points and long-track completeness +8 points over two_phase with holistic rounds, velocity error not higher; on wp2: leave-one-camera-out error not higher, and clearly more tracks of 50 frames or more.

    D3. Identity-swap repair and the overlap-window solver (sections 3-4) - Zig-zag detector, re-assignment through the k2 record, and the window solver. - Gate: on truth, swaps per 1000 track frames down by half and tracks through overlaps continuous in at least 80 % of cases; on wp2, acceleration flatness (M6) lower.

    D4. Density ladder - Run the whole chain at 0.005 -> 0.04 ppp against trackcorr and two_phase. Find the density at which yield or velocity error break down, and which part breaks first. That density is the recommended seeding limit, and the part that breaks is the next thing to fix.

    D5. Real data - wp2 (300 frames, then all 1072) and lv-multi on scratch copies: leave-one-camera-out error, tracks of 50 frames or more, acceleration flatness, and the Eulerian statistics against the MATLAB reference.

    D6. Only if D4 shows it is needed: small-window image re-reads - Around a prediction, re-detect with a local threshold or fit the known particle image to a 9x9 window. Still no global image loop.

    8. Speed budget¶

    Detection at most +20 % (moments in the existing loop, fits on doubtful blobs only). Matching and tracking at most +50 %, since alternatives are consulted only at conflict sites. Whole pipeline at most 1.5x today at 0.005 ppp, measured at every rung of the density ladder.

    D1. The hypothesis detector, scored against rendered truth (measured 2026-11-21; first pass)¶

    Code: src/openptv2/blob_hypotheses.py (cues from each blob's own pixels, one- and two-Gaussian fits, a small logistic regression), scripts/d1_features.py, d1_split.py, d1_classify.py, d1_faint.py. Tests in tests/unit/test_blob_hypotheses.py. The reference segmentation (segmentation._vectorized_targ_rec) gained an optional pixels_out to hand out each blob's pixels; it finds exactly the blobs of the production detector (1298 against 1298 in the check frame). The production Cython detector is not changed yet. Rendered syn1, 4 cameras, frames 1/5 for training and 9/13 for testing; blobs are labelled by how many true tracers fall inside them.

    Which cue says "more than one tracer" (single cues, area under the ROC curve; 0.5 = nothing): major-axis variance 0.82, eccentricity 0.77, pixel count 0.75, summed grey value 0.71, mean intensity 0.63, internal maxima 0.62, third moment 0.52. So mean intensity is a weak cue for overlap, as expected: overlap shows in size and shape, and the signature of a tracer should stay sumg / n only as a brightness identity, not as the overlap detector. Combined with the gain of the two-Gaussian fit over the one-Gaussian fit, the logistic model reaches 0.87.

    Calibration of p_single on the test frames: predicted against actual share of "two or more" in six bins agrees to within 0.05 in five of them; the top bin (predicted 0.94, 113 blobs) has 0.79 actually, 0.15 off. At p above 0.3 the model catches 67 % of two-tracer blobs with 9 % false alarms among single-tracer blobs; at p above 0.5, 48 % with 3.6 %.

    Centre of the split (two-tracer blobs, true count given, single-tracer variance 0.4 px² from the fit on clean blobs, where the Gaussian fit gives a 0.06 px centre error):

    true separation blob centroid error (px, median) fitted centre error fitted within 0.5 px
    under 1 px 0.28 0.35 0.67
    1-2 px 0.75 0.24 0.71
    2-3 px 1.17 0.42 0.54
    over 3 px 1.68 0.69 0.42
    all (600 blobs) 0.67 0.33 0.62

    Faint candidates (peaks at a lower threshold that are not within 3 px of a standard blob; 1114 true tracers had no standard blob in the test frames): threshold 3 recovers 35 % of them with 1.5 false candidates per recovered one; threshold 5 recovers 27 % with 1.2; threshold 7 recovers 17 % with 1.0.

    Against the gate - Split centres within 0.5 px for at least half of the two-tracer blobs: met (0.62), with the true count given. - p_single calibrated within 0.1: met in five of six bins, not in the top bin. - Faint candidates: at least a third of the missing tracers with fewer than 2 false per recovered: met at threshold 3 (0.35 and 1.5), narrowly. A faint candidate by itself is not trustworthy; it is only used where a prediction or an epipolar line asks for one (section 3), which removes most of the false ones. - Split centre error better than the 0.27 px track prediction: not met on its own (median 0.33; better only for separations of 1-2 px). The split is an independent measurement, so combined with the prediction the error falls to about 0.2 px if the two errors are independent, and the blob centroid it replaces is off by 0.67 px. D2 is the real test of that, and its stop rule applies.

    Caveats. All of this is on rendered images in which the tracer is a Gaussian of known width, so it is optimistic for real data, where the tracer image is smaller (0.25-0.28 px², D0) and its shape is less regular. The classifier trained on rendered data cannot be transferred as it is; on a real run the cue weights have to come from that run (the tracks themselves give labels: a blob a good track passes through while a second track also predicts into it). Pixel count and the fits depend on the threshold and the particle image size, so they are re-estimated per camera and run (scripts/psf_estimate.py).

    Decision. Go on to D2 with the split as an extra, weighted measurement at conflict sites and faint candidates only where a prediction asks for them; D2's gate decides whether it stays.

    Where the holistic loop spends its time (profile, 2026-11-21)¶

    two_phase + holistic_rounds=2 on the D0 case, 200 frames, cProfile (172 s under the profiler, 157 s without):

    part seconds what it is
    refine_merged (2 rounds) 82 per-observation merged-blob refinement; 836,664 calls of split_blob_effective (28 s), project_pixels (22 s), 5.6 million sig_of calls, the rest is Python loop overhead
    triangulate_weighted 29 1,600 calls (frame by camera set), each a weighted least squares
    relink 28 two_phase run again, 4 times, about 7 s each: the same cost as the base tracker
    birth_targets 11 particles hidden in other particles' blobs
    base two_phase and setup about 58 the 8 s tracker, store I/O and the rest

    Nearly all of the loop is scalar Python called once per observation (250,000 per round), where each observation is independent of the others. That is the shape that vectorises, and compiles, well. A first estimate is that batching split_blob_effective, the projection and the weighted triangulation over a frame at a time in NumPy brings the 82 + 29 s to a few seconds, and Cython kernels for the three hot functions to about one. The four relinks cost what four two_phase runs cost and shrink only if the rounds are made incremental (relink only the tracks that changed). Target: whole loop at most 2x two_phase before D2 builds on it.
