    Particle trackers in openptv2¶

    Three trackers, two switches, one add-on. That is the whole setup. This page is the user guide; the machine-readable version is openptv list-trackers (src/openptv2/tracking_registry.py), and the single place that interprets a saved configuration is src/openptv2/tracking_setup.py. For measured numbers see Tracker Tutorials; for files and pipeline see the Tracking Guide; for the default tracker in depth, Two-Phase Tracking; for the add-on, Holistic tracking.

    Quick start¶

    Do nothing and you get the recommended setup: two_phase, direction: both, no extras. For difficult data (overlapping particles) openptv recommend <run.zarr> measures your points and says which extras are worth their run time (Choosing).

    track:
  tracker: two_phase      # two_phase | trackcorr | fast_3d          default: two_phase
  direction: both         # forward | both                           default: both
  postprocess: true       # trackcorr, fast_3d: reciprocity + gaps   default: true
  holistic_rounds: 0      # two_phase add-on: merged-blob loop       default: 0 (off)
  shared_correction: false  # two_phase add-on: fix points in shared blobs  default: false
  dvxmin: -2.0            # search bounds, same for every tracker
  dvxmax: 2.0             #   (dvy*, dvz* likewise) mm/frame
  dacc: 0.4               # mm/frame^2
  angle: 120              # gon
    Where How
    GUI Tracking Parameters: Tracker, Direction, Post-process, the bounds, and the add-on box Merged-blob loop rounds. Saved into the YAML above.
    Batch openptv-batch parameters_Run1.yaml 1 500 uses the YAML. Override on the command line: --tracker fast_3d, --direction forward, --holistic-rounds 2, --shared-correction. The same flags exist in the parallel batch.
    Python run_tracking_plugin("default", exp) reads the YAML; run_tracking_plugin("trackcorr", exp) names the tracker.
    Advice openptv recommend res/run.zarr: measures the data, names the tracker and the extras, with reasons.
    List openptv list-trackers (table); openptv list-trackers --show two_phase (details and every setting).

    Choosing: which tracker, which extras¶

    Short answer: two_phase, with nothing else, unless your data says otherwise. The extras (ptv.shared_pass, track.shared_correction) are for data where particles overlap in the camera images. They bring a solid but not drastic improvement and cost run time, so they are never on by default.

    Ask your own data: openptv recommend <run.zarr> measures six numbers on your points (no truth needed) and prints the setup below with the reasons. The numbers: points per frame; spacing/step (how many steps apart neighbours are); mutual-link share (how many points have an unambiguous partner in the next frame; low = dense); noise/step (the velocity kink of a track against its step: 0 = exact data); partial views (share of points seen by fewer cameras than the rig has); shared-blob flag (share of points with one view much brighter than their other views, the sign of a blob holding two particles).

    Your data Use Why (benchmark, 13 cases; real wp1, wp2 for the descriptors)
    Real or noisy data (any density, any step) two_phase fastest (4 to 5 s per 200 frames, against 25 to 176 s for trackcorr) and the most accurate: velocity error 1.3 to 7 times lower than trackcorr or fast_3d; the only one that copes when neighbours are fewer than 10 steps apart
    Exact data (simulation, no noise: noise/step below 0.1, no merged blobs) fast_3d keeps every point (yield 0.995) where two_phase's ghost rules drop 3 to 6 % of them; trackcorr gives the same result 2 to 3 times slower
    You must reproduce a legacy (OpenPTV, C trackcorr) result trackcorr the legacy algorithm. It is never the fastest here, it is the least accurate on noisy data (velocity error 0.20 to 1.05 against 0.08 to 0.41 for two_phase), and with large steps it breaks (frame skip 4: 0.39 against 0.11; skip 8: 0.82 against 0.13, 176 s)
    Shared-blob flag 10 % or more two_phase + shared_correction (+ shared_pass) see below; real recordings wp1 and wp2 have 15 %
    Shared-blob flag below 8 % two_phase alone the extras change nothing (clean data) or a few percent

    What tracking is¶

    A camera takes a picture every frame. In each picture, every particle is a dot. In the next picture the dots have moved a little. Tracking means deciding which dot in picture 2 is the same particle as each dot in picture 1.

    When particles are far apart and slow, this is easy: take the nearest dot. It gets hard when particles cross each other, when one is hidden for a frame, or when positions are noisy. Every tracker below is a different set of rules for that decision. They differ in only four things:

    1. Guess — where do you expect the particle next? (stay put, constant velocity, smoothed curve, look at future frames, bigger time step)
    2. Score — how do you rank candidates? (distance, acceleration, angle, camera-image agreement)
    3. Conflicts — two particles want the same dot: who wins? (first come, cheapest first, best total pairing, nobody)
    4. Evidence — what do you look at? Only 3D points, or also the original camera images? Trackers that check the images survive bad 3D points; trackers that ignore them are faster.

    Two facts shape everything on this page. First, measured on synthetic flow (see docs/tracking-benchmark-results.md), most wrong links come from missing detections across gaps (~60%) and cold starts (~20%) — not from the scoring rule. So gap handling matters more than clever costs. Second, particles only ever enter and exit the volume; a track that ends mid-volume is almost always an occlusion or a dropout, not a real exit.

    The three trackers¶

    two_phase (default) trackcorr fast_3d
    In one line 3D candidates, image-based cost, optimal pairing, confirmation The legacy algorithm: search the 2D targets in each camera Links the triangulated 3D points only
    Looks at 3D points and the 2D blob positions in every camera 2D targets in the images and 3D 3D points only (optional soft epipolar score)
    Predicts position + velocity × steps, velocity learned along the track the last step repeated the last step repeated (neighbours' velocity at track starts)
    Picks links best total pairing per group of conflicting candidates (Hungarian) per particle, in the legacy order cheapest link first, per pass (greedy)
    Checks a link must be continued smoothly by the next one (two-hop) epipolar match in all cameras, acceleration and angle limits acceleration limit
    Gaps carried inside the loop (max_gap) bridged in post-processing bridged in post-processing
    Choose it for quality and speed on the standard pipeline; the only one the add-on works with comparison with the legacy software, old results runs with triangulated points only; a 3D-only baseline
    Needs the run store (the standard pipeline) the targets of every camera triangulated points

    How to choose. Start with the default. Use trackcorr when you must reproduce the legacy software. Use fast_3d when you have only triangulated points, or want a baseline that ignores the images. Each tracker has its own few extra settings, listed below; do not carry them from one tracker to another.

    What we measured¶

    One synthetic case with truth (1,220 particles per frame, 200 frames, real-like merge rate and brightness scatter; scripts/synth_bench.py, case L1_d1_c0_k1_n200_s0.08_a0.18_f0.05_m0.75), same search bounds for all, run time on one machine with nothing else running. Velocity error = RMS of the smoothed velocity error against the true flow, relative to the rms speed (lower is better); link precision = share of links that are true.

    Tracker, direction velocity error link precision long-track completeness time
    two_phase, both (default) 0.105 0.971 0.62 4.0 s
    two_phase, forward 0.116 0.973 0.59 4.2 s
    fast_3d, both 0.155 0.940 0.67 9.8 s
    fast_3d, forward 0.157 0.941 0.67 4.9 s
    trackcorr, both 0.220 0.940 0.54 24.2 s
    trackcorr, forward n/a n/a n/a 18.2 s

    Notes. (1) Writing the results to the run store used to dominate fast_3d and trackcorr (every frame wrote several zarr arrays; fast_3d took 22.6 s and trackcorr 34.6 s, with the kernel itself about 2 s for fast_3d). Tracking now holds the linkage in memory and writes it once per pass, rewrites in place, and skips identical re-writes: fast_3d forward 11.3 s to 4.9 s, trackcorr both 34.6 s to 24.2 s, with identical link quality. What remains for trackcorr is its 2D search itself. (2) With fast_3d, forward alone and both directions are close (velocity error 0.157 and 0.155 here; the other cases below); two_phase gains more from both.

    Direction, three cases (velocity error; both for fast_3d is the reversed pass, see below):

    Case two_phase fwd / both fast_3d fwd / both (reversed) / both (old C-style backward) trackcorr fwd / both
    default merge rate 0.188 / 0.183 0.254 / 0.243 / 0.265 0.294 / 0.297

    One more finding: on a clean dense case with no ghost points (L0_d4_c0_k1_n150, 4 times the seeding) two_phase keeps 94 % of the true points where fast_3d and trackcorr keep 99.6 %. So they stay on; switch them off (q_seed: null, q_young: 0) for data you know has no ghost points.

    Settings: shared, and per tracker¶

    Setting two_phase trackcorr fast_3d
    dvx*, dvy*, dvz* (mm/frame) yes (as the radius v_max: the largest of the six) yes yes
    dacc (mm/frame²), angle (gon) no: it confirms links instead (confirm_tol, automatic) yes yes
    direction, postprocess direction yes; postprocess not used (it carries gaps and confirms inside the loop) both both
    holistic_rounds yes (the add-on) no no (tried, see below)
    leaf_weight, max_gap, confirm_*, q_*, blob_gate yes no no
    epi_weight, accel_gate no no yes (defaults 0 and off)

    fast_3d reads dacc and angle only when accel_gate: true is set. Measured: with a loose dacc (6) the velocity error fell from 0.1665 to 0.1595 and link recall stayed at 0.999; with a tight dacc (0.4) link recall fell to 0.887, so take dacc from a probe run. epi_weight (the old "hybrid" penalty) changed the velocity error by 0.0004 (0.1665 to 0.1661); the old "hybrid" never applied it at all (a bug, fixed), so its measured results were plain fast_3d.

    The two switches¶

    direction: forward | both. Forward tracks the frames in order. both also tracks backward and keeps what is consistent or fills gaps. It is two passes, so it takes longer, and it gives the better trajectories for two_phase. How backward is done depends on the tracker:

    • two_phase and fast_3d: the same tracker on the reversed frame numbers (backward is only a different way of numbering the frames), then the forward links stay and backward links are added where both ends are still free (Forward-First). For fast_3d this beat the old C-style backward on all three benchmark cases.
    • trackcorr: the legacy backward pass (trackback), because the tracker is meant to match the legacy software; a reversed pass was measured and was worse for it (velocity error 0.327 against 0.297).

    In the GUI the Track back menu item exists for trackcorr and fast_3d runs that were made forward-only.

    postprocess: true | false. After the passes: reciprocity check (a link must be found from both ends) and gap bridging. For trackcorr and fast_3d. two_phase does these inside its own loop, so the switch has no effect there.

    The add-on: holistic_rounds¶

    The merged-blob loop. Where two particles overlap in a camera image, the detector gives one blob with a shifted centre. The loop estimates a centre for each particle from its own track, re-triangulates, and links again; holistic_rounds: 2 repeats that twice. It costs run time. It works with two_phase; asking for it with another tracker is ignored with a log line. It was tried around fast_3d and made the velocity error worse (0.167 to 0.199: fast_3d has no link confirmation, and the extra points bring wrong links), so it is not offered there. Details and measurements: Holistic tracking.

    The add-on: shared_correction¶

    The part of the merged-blob loop that matters most, on its own and cheaper. A point that uses a blob another particle also uses has a shifted centre in that camera, and its position is wrong by a correlated amount that smoothing cannot remove (down-weighting such points in the smoothing gained 2 % even with perfect weights). With shared_correction: true the linker does this:

    1. link once (as usual);
    2. for every point that touches a shared blob (or whose blob is too bright for its own particle), take the track's predicted position of the other members of the blob and compute this point's own share of the blob centre, (centre - sum of the others' weights x predicted positions) / its own weight; weights are the brightness signatures of the tracks. Triangulate from those corrected views, against the track's path as a prior. Points that touch no shared blob are not touched;
    3. link again with the corrected positions. The second link is needed: it is what lets the linker connect the corrected points (link recall 0.974 to 0.984 on the benchmark); keeping the old links with new positions was measurably worse.
    track:
  shared_correction: true     # two_phase only; default false. Batch: --shared-correction

    If no blob is shared by two points the step is skipped. It works best with ptv.shared_pass (the new points of the pass are then corrected too and are as smooth as the others). Cost: about 4 times the two_phase run time (two link passes plus the correction).

    Before tracking: the shared-blob pass (ptv.shared_pass)¶

    Two particles that overlap in a camera produce one blob. The stereo matcher uses every blob once, so the first particle takes it and the second is left with its other cameras, often only two, which the matcher does not accept. On the benchmark that costs a particle seen in 4 cameras with 2 of them shared about a third of its chances to be found (64 % found, against 100 % when no blob is shared). The tracker cannot get back a point that was never made.

    shared_pass is an option of the matching step (the ptv section of the parameter file, not track):

    ptv:
  shared_pass: true      # or a number, the brightness ratio (default 1.7); false / absent = off (default)

    After the normal matching, the blobs that look shared are offered again together with the targets no point used, and the matcher runs once more on that small set. A blob looks shared when it is brighter than the ratio times the second-dimmest view of the same matched point (grey sums normalised by the camera's median blob). A new point needs at least 3 cameras, at least 2 of them on blobs nobody else used, and its shared views count little in the position. It costs about 10 % more matching time and changes nothing when no blob is shared (clean data).

    Measured with two_phase (benchmark, ratio 1.7; yield, long-track completeness, velocity error):

    Case yield long tracks velocity error
    real-like merge rate (m0.75) 0.754 -> 0.772 0.623 -> 0.658 0.1046 -> 0.1016
    default merge rate 0.680 -> 0.700 0.503 -> 0.526 0.1825 -> 0.1832
    4x density 0.227 -> 0.236 0.182 -> 0.189 0.433 -> 0.445
    no merged blobs no change no change no change

    The new points are noisier than ordinary ones (about 3 times the position error), so the gain is in yield and continuity; at 4x density it costs a little velocity error and a few more ghost points, so use it for sparse to moderate seeding. Combined with holistic_rounds it supplies most of what the births do, and the loop's position refinement then lowers the error further. Measurements: docs/plans/2026-11-21-tracker-setup.md, sections 14 and 15.

    Old names still work (for one release)¶

    A saved YAML or script that uses an old name is translated by tracking_setup.resolve_tracking_setup, and the log says what it became. Old names will stop working after one release of warnings.

    Old Now
    priority_segment_3d, fast, fast_3d, cython_3d*, openptv_fast_3d fast_3d
    trackcorr (any engine key absent or epipolar), cython_epipolar*, openptv_epipolar trackcorr, the legacy algorithm only
    trackcorr with trackcorr_engine: 3d_hybrid (the old default) fast_3d with epi_weight: 1, direction both
    full_multipass, two_directional trackcorr, direction both
    standard_forward trackcorr, direction forward
    holistic two_phase, holistic_rounds: 2
    unified, 4be, hybrid_deltat_3d, splitter_tracking retired; shown as the nearest kept tracker
    run_backward, backward, bidirectional, direction: forward_backward direction: forward \| both
    a plugin name of your own runs your plugin, unchanged

    What changed in behaviour. (1) The default is now two_phase with direction: both; before, a YAML without a choice ran the 3D-only tracker forward. (2) trackcorr now always means the legacy algorithm; before, it ran the faster 3D "hybrid" unless trackcorr_engine: epipolar was set. To keep the old default behaviour use tracker: fast_3d with epi_weight: 1. (3) New projects no longer get preset: full_multipass in the YAML.

The full frameworks (triangulation pipelines, calibration, backtracking/repair, smoothing toolboxes) live only in their own repositories — use those projects directly if you need them. Both are permissively MIT-licensed.

    • proPTV by Robin Barta and colleagues (DLR) — probabilistic PTV framework, Python. Repository: https://github.com/RobinBarta/proPTV · Paper: Barta, R. et al. (2024). proPTV: A probabilistic particle tracking velocimetry framework. Journal of Computational Physics, 514,
